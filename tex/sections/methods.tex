\section{Method}

\subsection{Substrates and ground truth}

Both substrates are Python functions with executable hidden test suites, so every stratum,
recall denominator and false-positive population is decided by an interpreter and no model judges
any outcome. \textbf{Substrate 1} is HumanEval+ and MBPP+ (EvalPlus), whose median task is 41
words of prose and 6 lines of reference code, with the benchmark's base suite shown to the generator
and its plus suite held back. \textbf{Substrate 2} is the standard-library half of BigCodeBench,
whose median task is 118 words and 41 lines. BigCodeBench ships one test class per task, so the
visible and hidden suites are separated here: two test methods chosen by a fixed seed are the
visible suite and all methods are the hidden suite, with the class run unmodified and told which
methods to execute. Tasks whose declared modules are unavailable in the frozen interpreter, or
whose reference solution fails its own suite there, are dropped before generation and counted.

Candidates are written by one generator shown the specification and the visible tests. A candidate
that passes the visible suite and fails the hidden one is a \textbf{defective} instance; one that
passes both we call \textbf{correct}; one that fails the visible suite is discarded. Recall is
measured on the defective stratum and false positives on a sample of the correct stratum.

Two things follow from those definitions and are meant literally throughout. \emph{Correct} means
\emph{passes the hidden suite}: it is a benchmark label produced by an interpreter, not a
guarantee that the program meets its specification, and a suite can be incomplete or wrong. Since
this paper's own third result is that a specification often fails to determine what the hidden
suite asks, we cannot also treat passing that suite as correctness in the ordinary sense, and a
\emph{false positive} is likewise a flag on test-passing code rather than a demonstrated mistake
by the auditor. And the defect population is not a sample of model-written code: it is the
\textbf{residue that already survived the visible tests}, which is by construction the harder
part. A recall figure here is the fraction of that residue an auditor catches, never the fraction
of all defects a model writes.

\subsection{The auditor and the estimand}

The auditor reads the specification, the visible tests and the candidate, and returns findings
with severities; an increment is blocked when any finding is graded BLOCKER. It never sees the
hidden suite. The estimand throughout is \textbf{union recall at $K$}: the fraction of defective
instances blocked by at least one of $K$ independent readings, averaged exactly over all
$\binom{K_{\max}}{K}$ subsets rather than by sampling. The matching quantity on the correct
stratum is the union false-positive rate. A union recall figure is reported only beside the
false-positive rate it cost.

Four auditor routes appear below. The \textbf{shipped cross-vendor} auditor is
\texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}; the \textbf{same-vendor} auditor is \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, the
generator's own family and size; the \textbf{stronger same-vendor} auditor is
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4.6}; and a third family, \texttt{gpt-6-astra} at high reasoning, is read
at $K=4$. Candidates on both substrates are written by \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}. Prompts, the
auditor's constitution and every capability card are in the repository.

The routes are \textbf{not sampled alike}, and this bounds every cross-family union-of-$K$
contrast in the paper. The cross-vendor route's capability card does not permit a sampling
temperature, so no sampling parameter is sent and the route varies between readings; the
same-vendor routes permit one and the layer sends temperature $0$, so those routes are
near-deterministic. A union over readings that do not differ cannot grow, so the two families'
unions are not equally free to rise with $K$, and a cross-family difference in union recall
confounds auditing ability with how much each union was permitted to grow. The asymmetry is a
property of the shipped routing layer we measured rather than a design choice, we did not discover
it until after the runs, and we report the $K=1$ contrast beside the $K=8$ contrast wherever it
matters. A temperature-matched replication is the first item in \S\ref{sec:limits}.

\subsection{Statistics}

The unit of observation is the instance and the resampling unit is the problem, because problems
recur across generation batches and instances sharing a problem are not independent. The $K$
readings of an instance are repeated measurements of it, not $K$ further independent observations.
The bootstrap over problems estimates uncertainty from which tasks the benchmark happens to
contain; it does not estimate how much a fresh batch of model calls on these same tasks would
move the numbers, which would need replicate runs we did not budget for at every arm. Every rate carries a 95\% percentile bootstrap over whole problem
clusters (10{,}000 resamples, seeds fixed in each preregistration), with the Wilson interval
printed beside it and marked as too narrow. Paired contrasts carry an exact McNemar test and a
cluster-level sign-flip permutation test; where every discordant pair points one way the
percentile bootstrap's bound at zero is an artefact and an exact unconditional interval is quoted
instead. A saturation curve is summarised by a constrained exponential fit, and the fitted
asymptote is quoted only when the preregistered flattening bar is met; otherwise it is labelled an
extrapolation and the raw union at $K_{\max}$ is the number quoted. Counts of five or fewer are
quoted as counts. Multiplicity is handled as a single Bonferroni family per study over every
comparison actually computed, not over those planned: in the study carrying the primary contrasts
that family has sixteen members and a threshold of $0.00313$, and two comparisons clear it. Interval
coverage is stated rather than assumed. In simulation the primary percentile bootstrap under-covers
by roughly 2 to 5 points; under the actual clustered design its coverage is unvalidated, so every
primary interval in this paper is of unverified coverage. We state this rather than assume the
construction is sound because two earlier paired-contrast constructions in this programme were
found by review to cover $0.416$ and $0.075$ where $0.95$ was claimed, and both were replaced. No sample size was
justified in advance by a minimum-detectable-effect calculation.

\textbf{An interval that contains zero is reported as one that does not exclude zero, and
nothing more.} No equivalence margin was registered anywhere in this programme and no
equivalence test was performed, so none of those intervals is evidence that two conditions are
alike or that an intervention did nothing --- a distinction this work has already had to make
the hard way, having once inferred equal cost from an interval containing zero and withdrawn it
on review. Where a hypothesis fails its registered criterion we say it failed, not that the
effect is absent.

All analysis is plain Python 3.13 using only the standard library --- \texttt{random},
\texttt{math}, \texttt{itertools}, \texttt{csv} and \texttt{json}. The bootstrap, the exact
McNemar test, the cluster sign-flip permutation and the exact subset enumeration behind every
union-at-$K$ figure are implemented in the repository rather than called from a numerical
package, so each is readable at the point where it is used, and none depends on a third-party
version.

\subsection{Preregistration and independent review}

Each study was preregistered before its first model call, fixing its population, hypotheses,
primary outcome, intervals, kill conditions, budget and stopping rule. Departures are numbered
amendments committed before the step they govern, each recording what was known at the time; where
an amendment followed an interim observation, the results say so and argue whether the change
could favour the hypothesis. Each report was then reviewed by a model from a different vendor with
read access to the raw records, asked to re-run the analysis, recompute the intervals
independently, and check every sentence against the records. Reviews are archived verbatim, including those that
refused approval, and several studies needed many rounds: on one day five reports were refused
at once, and clearing those five took thirty-one further rounds. \emph{No measurement moved in
any of them.} What moved was what the reports claimed about their measurements --- and in most
of the later refusals the object of the refusal was a repair written to satisfy the previous
round, not the study it repaired. We state that because it is the most transferable thing this
process produced: a fix written to satisfy a reviewer is itself an unreviewed claim.

\textbf{The reviewer is a language model, and this paper is about the ceiling on language
models reading code they did not write.} That applies to our own instrument, and the round
counts are the evidence: the studies here cleared review at rounds \textbf{4, 6, 7, 9, 10 and
11}, and in every case the round before the last still returned substantive findings. A single
pass with high recall would clear a report in one round or two. So \emph{quotable at round
$N$} means a report survived $N$ passes, not that it was verified, and the marks in this paper
should be read as a filter of unknown recall rather than as proof.

Two things sharpen that. First, the split: across every round of every study, \emph{no
measurement ever moved}. The reviewers reproduced rates, intervals, subset enumerations and
seeds independently and found no arithmetic error, and every refusal was about what the prose
claimed of arithmetic already confirmed. On recomputation the instrument is strong; on
interpretation it is weak, and the two should not be quoted as one assurance. Second, the class
of defect this leaves open is the one that looks right to a language model. The clarification
study audited the wrong program for 384 readings --- a per-problem field used where a
per-instance one was needed --- and every automated gate passed, because each checked that the
code was \emph{identical across conditions} and it was, identically wrong. It was found by hand,
reading an adjudication sheet and noticing that a displayed program returned exactly what its
hidden suite was said to reject. Neither the gates nor a reviewer found it, and we do not know
what else of that kind remains.

\textbf{Every review prompt in this programme stated the claimed result before the reviewer
began, and we tested what that cost.} One report that had been declared quotable at round 6,
after five refusals and unchanged since, was reviewed once more under a prompt that supplied the
artefacts, the question and the verification list but \emph{withheld every figure, every
population description and every prior verdict}, and asked for an independent computation
first. The verdict agreed, and all six contrasts reproduced at a seed and resample count the
reviewer chose before reading ours. \textbf{Six substantive findings did not agree}: among them
that the report's two groups were not one population with duplicate rows but two different
definitions of \emph{caught}; that its rating sheet displays at most three failing inputs while
81 of its 121 entries have more recorded; that a count of changed labels was 71 where the data
say 67; and that one comparison we drew was uninterpretable because two of its three terms had
changed together. Six briefed rounds on the same file had found none of them. \textbf{Telling a
reviewer what we claim appears to narrow what it examines}, so the first round of every review
after this one is dispatched with the conclusions withheld. We report this because a paper
measuring a ceiling on automated auditing, which then audits itself, owes the reader the one
check of its own instrument it had not run.

Every study drawn on here has completed that review. One further study was re-run twice, closed
without a quotable number, and appears nowhere in this paper except in this sentence.
