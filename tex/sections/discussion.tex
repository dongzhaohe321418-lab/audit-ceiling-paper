\section{Discussion}
\subsection{What a union-recall number means}

The caution this subsection argued --- that a union-recall figure is contingent on the rate it
costs, so recall quoted without its false-positive rate is not portable --- rested on a second
substrate that was re-run twice and closed without a quotable number
(\S\ref{sec:inreview}). We state the caution as a
methodological point rather than as a measured result, because the argument for it does not need
our second substrate: recall and false positives move together with any severity threshold, and a
critic can be tuned to any recall by blocking more. What our records still support directly is the
narrower version of it on the substrate we report, where the same auditor at two severity rules sits at
two different operating points (\S\ref{sec:auditor}), and where the shipped auditor's 30.0\%
[20.0, 40.7] recall is quoted only beside the 16.0\% [10.1, 22.3] it cost.

The cleaner experiment for the general claim is a severity sweep within one substrate. Our
sweep re-grades archived findings at four decision rules and shows each family's recall and
false-positive rate moving with the rule (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}); because it re-grades rather
than re-asks, it cannot place the families at a common operating point. Comparing two substrates
was never that experiment: they differ in
specification length, solution length, problem population, defect distribution and suite
construction at once.

\subsection{Repeated reading buys coverage only where the decision is marginal}

Union-of-$K$ is the instrument this paper uses to find a ceiling, and it is worth saying plainly
what it cannot measure. Part of that needs no experiment at all: a union over readings that do not
differ cannot grow, so a union-of-$K$ curve is flat for a route that returns the same verdict
however often it is asked. That is arithmetic, not a finding.

The empirical illustration we had for it came from the second substrate, which closed without
a quotable number (\S\ref{sec:inreview}), so we no longer offer one. What survives is the design fact
behind it, which our own records do establish: the same-vendor routes are sent temperature $0$
while the cross-vendor route is sent no sampling parameter at all (\S\ref{sec:auditor}), and over
$K = 1$ to $8$ the first family's union gains 3.0 points against the second's 19.3. How much of
that difference is sampling and how much is the operating point, we cannot say, because no
temperature-matched arm was run. We report it as a confound on our own contrasts rather than as a
result about auditors.

What follows for best-of-K, union-of-K or panel aggregation over critics is only the arithmetic:
a route whose verdict does not vary between readings gains nothing by being read again. We
measured per-instance flag counts, not any distance to a decision boundary, and ran no panel
aggregation, so we offer no law for how much a varying route gains. Nor is the diminishing shape
itself evidence of a ceiling: exact subset averaging makes each reading's marginal gain no
larger than the last by construction, and what the curves measure is the size of those gains.

\subsection{What the ceiling is made of}

Repetition is the smallest of the contributions we could measure. Eight readings of one auditor buy
19.3 points of union recall, and twenty readings across three families leave 51.8\% [40.0, 63.3]
of the defect population untouched --- the complement of the pooled 48.2\% [36.7, 60.0]. Changing which model reads moves the same quantity --- union recall at $K=8$
over the same 110 instances --- by 26.4 points, though \S\ref{sec:auditor} gives three reasons not
to read that as auditing ability. Changing one sentence of the rulebook moves flags by 26.8 points,
but on 56 instances at one reading, and we do not place that number on the same scale as the other
two.

A fourth contribution is not the auditor's at all, and we can only describe it post hoc. Under a
rule that asks whether the specification's prose determines the hidden suite's expected value, 44
of the 57 never-flagged defects fail that test. We attempted a constructed population where the prose
does determine the answer; review established that no filter in it enforces that property, and
what it licenses is descriptive only (\S\ref{sec:inreview}). The association between never being flagged and never being determined is
empirical, not definitional --- selecting the missed instances does not fix their labels, and
against caught instances the third rater's post-hoc contrast is $+23.3$ points $[+2.0, +43.6]$
--- but selection and confounding prevent a causal reading, so it is an observation about where
the misses sit and not an estimate of how much the benchmark contributes. One intervention changes the specification instead of conditioning on it
(\S\ref{sec:inreview}): adding a behavioural rule to the prose moved diagnosis on 9 of 32 such
instances, all of them where the added rule agreed with the hidden suite and none of the 23
where it contradicted it. It is the first evidence here that manipulates the specification instead of
conditioning on it, though only within a population selected for having been missed and called
undetermined, and it cannot separate a specification that settles the question from one that
settles it correctly.

The constructed population carries a limitation we measured rather than assumed, and a second
one that review established. A frontier model separates its edits from natural ones at 96.7\%
on the items it answered, between 79.9\% and 97.3\% once the items it would not classify are
counted at either extreme --- \emph{by a feature it does not identify}, since the two arms
share no problem and the model is one of the two that gated the population. And the construction
does not enforce specification-determinedness at all, so it licenses \emph{no} bound on the
measured ceiling. We had written that it did. It does not.

\subsection{Flagging is not naming}

An audit that blocks an increment for the wrong reason has still blocked it, and a recall number
counts that as a catch. With the finding texts kept for one reading of the shipped auditor and
adjudicated blind by two raters, the auditor returned a finding on 9 of 110 defective instances
and asserted the actual failing behaviour on 5 to 7 of them, depending on how the two raters are
combined. The raters agreed at $\kappa$ = 0.391, which we report beside the rate it produces, because a
moderate agreement is part of what the rate is worth. That study's preregistered kill condition
fired on this result, and \S\ref{sec:inreview} states its headline in the form the kill requires.
The figure is a property of one reading and is not a naming rate for the eight-reading union;
establishing that would take adjudicating eight readings' texts, which were not archived.

\subsection{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The confound we would fix first is that auditor families are not sampled alike: one route is sent
temperature $0$ and the other no sampling parameter at all, which bounds how far one union can
grow before any difference in ability is measured. Every cross-family union-of-K contrast here
inherits it. Second, no auditor is compared with another at a matched false-positive rate; our sweep
re-grades archived findings at four decision rules, which cannot move a family to a point its
findings do not reach --- one has none between 3.3\% and 34.0\% false positives --- so the paper
cannot say whether the families differ once brought to the same operating point. Third, the primary intervals are
problem-cluster percentile bootstraps over 56 clusters; for single rates we measured their
coverage by simulation, near nominal at the headlines' rates and anticonservative below about
$0.10$ (\S\ref{sec:stats}), and for the paired contrasts and conditional shares it is
unmeasured, and no sample size was justified in advance by a detectable-effect
calculation. Fourth, the substrate is short, self-contained Python functions with executable
hidden tests, so nothing here measures auditing a change to a large program with no oracle, and
both of its benchmarks are old enough that memorisation cannot be ruled out. Fifth, the
generator is a single model, so the defect population is one model's failure
mode. Sixth, the residual rubric's raters were a model and the author, who wrote the rubric and was
not blind to the earlier labels; one external label set agrees with them across eleven overlapping
tasks. Seventh, our attempt to build a population of
specification-determined defects did not enforce the property, and its edits are separable from
natural code at 96.7\% [79.9, 97.3] by something the probe does not identify. Each of
these is recorded in the study that carries it, together with the number it would change.

\subsection{What we would do next}

The severity sweep we ran re-grades archived findings under four rules and shows the auditor
contrast is rule-dependent (\S\ref{sec:auditor}); it cannot reach a matched operating point for
every family, because re-grading only moves a cut through findings already produced, and one
family has no reachable point between 3.3\% and 34.0\% false positives. Re-asking rather than
re-grading --- instructing each family at graded severity and reading afresh --- is the
experiment this paper most needs, since only that can place the families at a common
false-positive rate and decide whether the auditor contrast survives there. Replicating the cross-family arms with the
sampling parameters matched would remove the confound above, and extending the cross-vendor family
past eight readings would turn a statement about our budget into a statement about a curve. We have run a first version of the experiment we most wanted --- hold the code and the visible
tests fixed and vary only the specification (\S\ref{sec:inreview}) --- and it left two gaps that
decide what it means. Its clarifier inferred the missing rule from failing inputs and was wrong
on most instances, so the rule's correctness was measured rather than manipulated; writing the
rule from the oracle for some instances and a deliberately wrong rule of matched length for
others would separate a specification that settles the question from one that settles it
correctly. And its manipulation check did not show the placebo inert; a placebo rated no more
determining than the original would. Both are cheap, because the code, the tests and the
adjudication rubric already exist. Beyond
that: adjudicating more than one reading of the shipped auditor would let a naming rate be compared
with a union flag rate at the same K; a second generator would show whether the residual's
character is a property of the benchmark or of one model's failures; a population of natural
defects independently known to be specification-determined would separate the injected population's
artificiality from the property it was built to isolate; and a substrate whose ground truth is not
a hidden test suite would test the assumption every ceiling here rests on, which is that a test
suite asked the right question.
